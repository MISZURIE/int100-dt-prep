## What is Design Thinking?

**Design Thinking** is a **human-centered** approach to problem solving. You start by understanding real users, then define the problem, generate options, build prototypes and test them, iterating until the solution meets users' needs.

The course textbook is the **Design Thinking Bootleg** from the d.school (Hasso Plattner Institute of Design, Stanford).

## The five stages

| Stage | Key question | Example output |
|---|---|---|
| **Empathize** | Who is the user? What do they think, feel, do? | Interview notes, empathy map |
| **Define** | What is the real problem? | POV statement, HMW questions |
| **Ideate** | What options do we have? | A large list of ideas, selected ideas |
| **Prototype** | How can we make the idea tangible? | Paper model, storyboard, role-play, mockup |
| **Test** | How do users respond? What did we learn? | Feedback capture grid, improvements |

> The process is **non-linear**. A team can return to an earlier stage at any time, e.g. test results may send you back to Empathize.

## Design thinker mindsets (d.school)

- **Focus on human values** — keep people and their values at the center
- **Show, don't tell** — communicate with things people can see and touch
- **Craft clarity** — distill a messy problem into a clear, actionable one
- **Embrace experimentation** — build to think, rather than thinking until done
- **Be mindful of process** — know which stage you're in, which method, and why
- **Bias toward action** — doing over talking
- **Radical collaboration** — work with people from diverse backgrounds

## Diverge and converge

Each stage has a **diverge** phase (gather as many inputs/options as possible) and a **converge** phase (select, synthesize, decide). Ideate is divergent; selecting ideas is convergent.

## INT100 at a glance

- 5 weeks, one stage per week, plus a **team project** across the semester
- All work goes into the **team GitHub repository** and is presented at **sharing sessions** for peer feedback
- The report covers every stage: Work (preparation and techniques), Results (artifacts), Peer Feedback, GitHub revisions and a **Retrospective**
- Grading: exam 60% · attendance/participation/class digest 15% · team project 25%

## Learning outcomes assessed by the exam

- **1A-I** Specify user needs using DT techniques
- **1C-I / 5UX-I** Propose a solution using DT techniques (Define and Prototype)
- **3C-I** Create a work plan and track work artifacts as a team

The exam focuses on **"describe and explain concepts, terms"**: you must explain what each technique is, why it matters, and give an example of using it.
