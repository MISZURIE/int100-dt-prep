## What Empathize is and why it matters

**Empathize** is the work you do to understand people within the context of your design challenge: what they **do, say, think and feel**. It is the **foundation of a human-centered design process**.

Why it matters:
- The problems you are solving are **not your own**. They belong to your users.
- People often can't articulate what they need. Observing behavior reveals **latent needs** and **insights**.
- It reduces the team's **bias and assumptions**.

## Three modes of Empathize

| Mode | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Observe** | Watch users' behavior in their real context | Watch students queue at the canteen at noon |
| **Engage** | Talk to and interview users, scheduled or "intercept" | Interview students while they queue |
| **Immerse** | Experience what the user experiences | Buy lunch yourself at the busiest time |

## Interview for empathy

d.school guidelines:
- **Ask why**, even when you think you know the answer
- **Never say "usually"**. Ask about a specific instance or occurrence.
- **Encourage stories**, because stories reveal how users think
- **Look for inconsistencies** between what people say and what they do
- **Pay attention to nonverbal cues**
- **Don't be afraid of silence**
- **Don't suggest answers**, and phrase questions **neutrally**
- **Don't ask binary (yes/no) questions**
- **Keep questions short** (about 10 words) and **ask one question at a time**
- **Make sure you're prepared to capture**: interview in pairs, with one person asking and one taking notes

Typical flow: introduce yourself → introduce the project → build rapport → evoke stories → explore emotions → question statements → thank and wrap up.

## Extreme users

**Extreme users** are people who use something unusually much or little, or face special constraints. Their needs are **amplified**, revealing problems that mainstream users overlook. For a food-ordering system, talk to someone who never uses apps and someone who orders every meal.

## Synthesis tools

- **Empathy map** with four quadrants: **Say · Do · Think · Feel**. Look for **insights** in tensions between quadrants.
- **What? / How? / Why?** Observe what users do and how, then interpret why, moving from concrete to abstract.
- **Saturate & group** — put all findings on a wall and cluster them to find patterns.

## What is an insight?

An **insight** is a new understanding that wasn't obvious at first, usually the "why" behind a behavior. Example: "Students don't mind waiting; they worry about being late for their afternoon class."
